There are afternoons that don’t ask very much of you.

The room was the same room. The cup was the same cup, with the small chip near the handle that I keep forgetting about until my thumb finds it. Outside, something was being repaired. The sound came and went.

I had meant to do a number of things. They were written down somewhere, in the confident handwriting of a person who hadn’t yet had lunch.

Instead, I sat by the window.

## An inventory of almost nothing

A rectangle of light crossed the floor. Slowly enough that I didn’t see it move, only noticed, after a while, that it had arrived somewhere else.

Someone walked past carrying flowers wrapped in newspaper. A dog investigated a parked bicycle with the seriousness of someone checking an old address. My tea cooled, then became something I was drinking out of habit rather than enjoyment.

> Maybe an ordinary day is only ordinary because we haven’t looked at it closely enough.

I don’t think there’s a lesson in this. I’m suspicious of the need to turn every quiet moment into one.

There was an afternoon. I was in it. For a little while, I wasn’t trying to get to the next thing.

That feels worth writing down.
