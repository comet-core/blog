We set off at around 09:15 on 30 August 2026. 

The weather was clean—a solid morning sky with patches of soft grey clouds drifting in from the Arabian Sea, keeping the worst of the Mumbai heat at bay. On paper, a Sunday morning drive is supposed to be the city's gentlest hour. In practice, the tarmac always has a few surprises waiting for you.

## NH48 and the art of unexpected physics

Joining NH48, I settled into the right lanes. In city highway logic, staying on the right usually keeps you clear of the constant friction from left-hand exits and merging traffic.

I was cruising calmly when I caught movement on the left: a Maruti Dzire driver actively reversing down the highway shoulder. No hazard lights, no warning, just casually backing up against highway speeds. It’s the kind of moment that sends an instant chill through your spine and reminds you that in Mumbai, defensive driving isn’t a suggestion—it’s an ongoing survival instinct.

Further down on the Coastal Road, another character appeared. In a strictly monitored 60 km/h zone, I was holding a steady 57 km/h when a Kia Seltos driver decided he was Max Verstappen on a qualifying lap. He tore past in the dedicated Bus lane doing well over 80 km/h, apparently convinced that speed limits are merely decorative suggestions.

## The joy of the machine

Despite the anomalies, driving the Jeep Compass down that corridor was pure satisfaction.

Once you clear the bottleneck and hit the Bandra-Worli Sea Link, the car comes into its own. The steering has that reassuring, heavy mechanical heft that builds confidence with every kilometer. Setting the cruise control on the open spans while the sea stretches out on both sides, the Compass feels completely planted.

Diving into the undersea tunnel of the Coastal Road, I toggled the speed limiter on. The smooth asphalt, the rhythmic hum of the ventilation shafts, and the soft amber tunnel lighting made the passage effortless. It’s rare to find stretches in Mumbai where you can genuinely appreciate how well a car handles, but that run through the tunnel was one of them.

## Arriving at the water

We pulled into Colaba around 10:00.

I hadn't visited the Gateway of India in years. At 10:00 on a Sunday morning, the usual overwhelming tourist crowds hadn’t arrived yet. The promenade was pleasantly quiet, occupied mostly by a few morning walkers, a handful of families, and photographers setting up their gear.

Standing by the sea wall, the grey harbor water stretched out toward the horizon, dotted with cargo vessels and ferries bobbing gently against the jetties.

Right behind the plaza, the Taj Mahal Palace Hotel stood imposing against the sky. Seeing those red domes in person always brings a quiet pause, carrying the solemn memory of 26/11 and the quiet resilience of the city that refused to be broken.

We spent a while just taking in the sea air and the open space. After the rush of the highway, there is something deeply grounding about ending a drive where the land simply runs out into the ocean.
